## What Is Group-Scoped Access?

Group-scoped access is a collaborative authorization mode in the SoundAdam Lab Console that allows all members of a research group to operate within a shared gateway context. Rather than each user holding an isolated personal session, group members connect to a common session environment — enabling synchronized voice experiments, joint runbook visibility, and coordinated real-time interaction across the team.

Access to a group scope is managed by your research group lead, who is responsible for adding members and maintaining group-level permissions.

---

## Shared Gateway Context

When you activate group-scoped access, your Lab Console session connects to the group's shared gateway context. This has direct implications for how experiments behave:

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Common Session State" icon="circle-nodes">
    All group members connect to the same gateway instance. Session state — including active configurations, context parameters, and live pipeline settings — is visible and accessible to the entire group.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Collaborative Experiments" icon="flask">
    Voice experiments, duplex interactions, KWS runs, and speech enhancement tasks launched by any group member are executed within the shared context, making results available to all participants.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Shared Runbook" icon="scroll">
    Session events generated by every group member are written to the group runbook. This gives the team a unified view of experiment history, interaction logs, and pipeline activity.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Group Session Tokens" icon="key">
    Authorization tokens under group-scoped access are bound to the group identity rather than an individual user, enabling coordinated access without requiring individual re-authorization for each member.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

## Shared Runbook: What Your Team Sees

The group runbook is a shared, append-only log of all session events generated within the group context. When operating in group-scoped mode:

- **All members' activity is logged** — voice experiment results, gateway session starts and stops, KWS detections, and speech enhancement outputs from every member appear in the shared runbook.
- **Events are attributed** — each runbook entry includes the originating user's identity, so you can distinguish your own activity from that of other group members.
- **History is cumulative** — the runbook accumulates entries across all sessions within the group scope. Members who join mid-session can review prior activity from that session.

<Warning>
  Because the group runbook surfaces activity from all members, any session you start or experiment you run in group-scoped mode will be visible to your entire research group. Avoid running sensitive or incomplete experiments in the group context if you do not want them logged to the shared runbook. Use [personal access](/access/personal) for private testing.
</Warning>

## How to Join or Be Added to a Group

Group membership is managed by your research group lead. You cannot add yourself to a group — access must be explicitly granted.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Contact Your Group Lead">
    Reach out to your research group lead and request to be added to the group scope. Provide your registered Lab Console username or the email associated with your account.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Group Lead Grants Access">
    Your group lead will add your account to the group through the Lab Console admin panel. You will receive a notification — or a direct confirmation from your lead — once your group membership has been provisioned.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify Your Access">
    Log in to [https://soundadam.com](https://soundadam.com) and open the **Context Selector**. Your group name should appear as an available context option alongside your personal context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Activate Group-Scoped Context">
    Select your group from the Context Selector to begin operating in the shared gateway environment.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Switching Between Personal and Group-Scoped Context

You can switch between personal and group-scoped contexts at any time from within the Lab Console, as long as you hold valid authorization for both scopes.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the Context Selector">
    Locate the **Context Selector** in the top navigation bar or session setup panel. It displays your currently active context (personal or group name).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a Context">
    Click the selector to expand available contexts. Select **Personal** for an isolated session, or select your **group name** to switch to the shared gateway context.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Confirm the Switch">
    The console will reload the session environment for the selected context. Confirm that the session header reflects the new context before starting any experiments.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Switching context does not automatically end an active session in the previous context. If you have a running gateway session or active experiment, finalize or pause it before switching to avoid orphaned session state.
</Note>

## Collaboration Considerations

Operating in a shared gateway context introduces coordination requirements that do not apply to personal access. Keep the following in mind when working with your research group:

### Simultaneous Sessions

Multiple group members can hold active sessions within the group context at the same time. However:

- **Concurrent experiment runs** may interact if they share pipeline configurations or gateway resources.
- **Session state changes** made by one member — such as modifying a gateway configuration — are immediately visible to all active group members.
- **Event ordering** in the shared runbook is chronological, so rapid simultaneous activity from multiple members may interleave in the log.

### Shared Latency Budget

The group gateway context operates under a shared latency budget. If multiple members are running high-throughput real-time voice experiments or duplex interactions simultaneously, latency for all participants may be affected. Coordinate with your group to stagger intensive workloads where possible.

### Runbook Hygiene

Because all group members write to the same runbook, agree on naming conventions and annotation practices with your team before starting collaborative sessions. Well-labeled entries make post-session analysis significantly easier.

<Tip>
  Before starting a shared experiment, check in with your group members to confirm who is active in the group context and what they are currently running. A quick coordination message prevents conflicting session states, unexpected runbook noise, and unintended interference between simultaneous voice experiment pipelines.
</Tip>
